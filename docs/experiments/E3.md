# E3 - Structural convergence

Status: complete; Steps 1-8 complete for E3-r01-primary.

See [E3 results](../results/E3-results.md) for measurements after execution.

## Project-plan objective

The project plan defines E3 as structural convergence analysis. For each dated cohort, measure skill size, heading counts, recurring sections, templates, and related structural features to determine whether skills are converging on shared formats. Structural convergence is distinct from lexical phrasing convergence in E4 and semantic/topic convergence in E5.

## Research questions

1. How do skill sizes and structural components vary across the ten E0-valid monthly cohorts?
2. Which heading names, heading levels, section orders, front-matter keys, and block types recur most often?
3. Does the prevalence of recurring structures increase, decrease, or remain stable across cohorts?
4. Are apparent structural trends robust to copied content, catalog/template signals, bulk repositories, and location class?
5. Do later cohorts show a smaller set of increasingly common structural templates, or only a stable core with changing long tails?

## Scope and unit of analysis

- Primary unit: one distinct representative content identified by `file_sha` in the E0 parsed skills table.
- Primary population: all parseable representatives for overall structural summaries.
- Cohort population: representatives with an E0-valid first-commit month from October 2025 through July 2026; July is treated as partial.
- Sensitivity populations: copy-inclusive artifact occurrences and distinct representatives split by catalog, template, bulk-repository, and location signals.
- Primary structural input: parsed `fm_*` fields, `body_prose`, and reconstructed Markdown block/heading information from representative content.
- Rerun compatibility: retain the anonymized `file_sha` join key and versioned structural features so E6 can re-aggregate E3 by E2 authorship class and E1 language without reparsing the corpus.
- No repository-owner, author, or raw repository names will be written to committed tables or documentation.

## Structural representation

Each representative will be converted into an auditable structural record without retaining raw prose in committed outputs. Candidate fields are:

- total body characters, words, lines, paragraphs, and non-empty blocks;
- front-matter presence, key count, normalized key set, and required-key presence;
- heading count, heading-depth histogram, heading-level sequence, normalized heading-name sequence, and maximum depth;
- normalized section-name set and ordered section transitions;
- paragraph, unordered-list, ordered-list, block-quote, table, fenced-code, inline-code, and thematic-break counts;
- code-block character share and prose-block character share;
- structural template identifier derived from a versioned canonical serialization of the non-textual feature sequence.

Normalization must remove wording while preserving structure: heading names are lowercased and whitespace-normalized for section recurrence, variable values are excluded from template signatures, and heading order/depth remain available as separate features. Empty, parser-error, and structurally unparseable records receive explicit statuses rather than being silently dropped.

## Convergence definitions

E3 will report multiple complementary signals rather than one convergence score:

- feature prevalence: share of skills containing each structural feature;
- recurring-section prevalence: share containing each normalized section label;
- template concentration: number and share of skills covered by the most common structural signatures;
- structural entropy: entropy of template identifiers and selected feature distributions;
- cohort change: absolute and relative prevalence changes from the earliest to latest valid cohort, with counts and denominators;
- pairwise structural similarity: Jaccard similarity for feature sets and sequence-aware similarity for heading/section order where computationally feasible.

“Convergence” will mean increasing concentration or decreasing structural diversity under the same representation and population. A change in raw document size alone will not be interpreted as convergence.

## Execution plan

### Step 1 - inventory E0 inputs and freeze the population

Confirm the full parsed-skills schema, representative count, parser status counts, cohort field, and E0 flag/rank availability. Reconcile the cohort denominator with the E0 date audit. Record exclusions and missingness before computing structural features.

### Step 2 - implement the structural extractor

Add a tested structural extraction module/script that processes representatives in streaming batches. Reconstruct Markdown blocks and headings consistently with E0/E1 parsing decisions. Emit only anonymized identifiers, numeric features, categorical structural signatures, status fields, and the E0 cohort, location, catalog/template/bulk, and `repo_rank` metadata needed for aggregation and E6 reruns. Write each completed batch to a durable Parquet part, maintain a progress JSON and run log, and publish the final Parquet only after row-count and distinct-key checks pass. A completion marker is required before Step 2 is considered complete.

### Step 3 - validate structural extraction

Test front matter, nested headings, duplicate headings, lists, tables, block quotes, fenced code, malformed Markdown, empty bodies, and mixed block sequences. Manually inspect a small anonymized validation frame and compare feature counts against parser expectations. Confirm that raw skill text and identifying metadata are not written to the derived structural output.

### Step 4 - compute overall structural distributions

Report size distributions, heading counts/depths, front-matter key coverage, block-type distributions, section-name frequencies, section-order patterns, and structural-template frequencies. Include explicit denominators, unknown/excluded counts, and concentration/entropy summaries.

### Step 5 - compute cohort trends

Repeat the structural summaries for each of the ten E0-valid monthly cohorts. Report cohort sizes, feature prevalence, template concentration, entropy, and confidence intervals or bootstrap intervals where appropriate. Treat July 2026 as partial and do not interpret it as a complete-month comparison without qualification.

### Step 6 - test robustness to reuse and repository signals

Repeat the primary summaries for copy-inclusive occurrences and for distinct representatives after excluding or stratifying by catalog, template, bulk-repository, and location-class signals. Keep distinct-content results primary. Report whether convergence patterns persist after repeated content and high-volume repositories are controlled.

### Step 7 - concentration and similarity analysis

Measure top-template coverage, effective number of templates, entropy, and pairwise structural similarity using deterministic sampling or scalable aggregation. Use equal-sized cohort samples for cross-cohort pairwise comparisons so changes are not driven only by cohort size. Report computational sampling, caps, and uncertainty explicitly.

### Step 8 - report structural convergence and limitations

Distinguish stable common structure from genuine increasing convergence. Discuss parser and Markdown ambiguities, copied/template content, bulk-repository concentration, cohort-size differences, partial July coverage, and the fact that structural similarity does not establish common authorship or semantic equivalence. Do not interpret E3 as a language, authorship, phrasing, topic, or integration experiment.

## E6 rerun compatibility

The primary E3 execution will report corpus-wide and cohort-level structural results. E6 is expected to rerun E3-E5 split by the E2 authorship classifications and E1 language classes to test whether observed convergence persists across authorship/language strata. To support that rerun, E3 will:

- preserve one anonymized structural row per representative with stable `file_sha`, cohort, and E0 sensitivity fields;
- version the extractor and canonical structural-template serialization;
- retain countable feature columns rather than only final aggregate tables;
- define minimum-cell and unknown-history rules before E6 stratification;
- keep E2 authorship and E1 language as later join dimensions, not as inputs that alter the primary structural representation.

The E6 rerun must use the same E3 feature version and report small-cell suppression, missing authorship/history, unknown language, and multiple-comparison or uncertainty decisions. It must not be interpreted as evidence that structure caused authorship or language differences.

## Acceptance criteria before E4

- Structural feature definitions and normalization are versioned and tested.
- Overall and cohort denominators reconcile with E0.
- Heading, section, block, front-matter, size, and template measures are reported separately.
- Convergence claims include concentration/entropy evidence and are not based on a single metric.
- Copy-inclusive, catalog/template/bulk, location, and cohort sensitivities are documented.
- July partial-cohort and parser/Markdown limitations are explicit.
- No raw skill text, author identities, repository-owner names, or repository names appear in committed E3 artifacts.

## Scope boundary

E3 measures structural form. Lexical phrasing and n-gram convergence belong to E4, topic coverage and semantic diversity belong to E5. E6 will rerun E3-E5 by E2 authorship class and E1 language before analyzing repository integration/moderation. Authorship evidence remains defined by E2 and is not redefined by E3.
