# E2 - Agent authorship and maintenance

Status: complete; Steps 1-8 executed. The empirical upper-bound classifier remains unavailable and is reported as a theoretical ceiling only.

See [E2 results](../results/E2-results.md) for results after the experiment is approved and run.

## Project-plan objective

The project plan defines E2 as estimating how many skills agents create or maintain by combining authorship markers. E2 must compare the authorship signal on the first commit with the signal on the last available commit so that creation and later maintenance are reported separately. Because commit histories are incomplete and many agent-written files may be committed under human identities, E2 must report a range rather than a single definitive count.

## Research questions

1. What lower-bound share of distinct skills has explicit evidence of agent involvement at creation?
2. What lower-bound share has explicit evidence of agent involvement in later maintenance?
3. How much do creation and maintenance classifications differ?
4. How do authorship estimates vary by E0 cohort, language, location class, copy/template status, and bulk-repository status?
5. How sensitive are the estimates to the marker definition, incomplete history, and repository-level concentration?

## Scope and unit of analysis

- Primary unit: one row per distinct representative `file_sha`.
- Primary population: representatives with usable commit history and an E0-valid cohort where cohort comparisons are required.
- Secondary populations: all representatives with any history, copy-inclusive artifact occurrences, and capped per-repository samples using E0 `repo_rank`.
- Creation: authorship evidence associated with the earliest available commit for the representative content.
- Maintenance: authorship evidence associated with the latest available commit, plus a separate indicator for any agent-like evidence between first and last commits.
- Unknown history is retained as an explicit category; no missing author or missing history value is treated as human authorship.

## Proposed authorship evidence model

E2 will preserve evidence sources separately before combining them. Candidate signals are:

1. Commit-identity markers: deterministic matches to documented bot, agent, automation, or model-account patterns in commit author metadata. Raw names, emails, and provider identifiers will not be written to committed outputs.
2. Commit-message markers: explicit generated-by, agent, model, automation, or workflow declarations in commit messages or trailers.
3. Content markers: explicit self-identification in the skill content, such as generated-by or agent-authorship declarations. These are evidence of a claim in the file, not proof of the commit identity.
4. Repository/workflow markers: explicit agent-generation configuration or workflow evidence available in the dataset's repository metadata. This signal is kept separate because it may indicate repository-level capability rather than authorship of a particular skill.

Each signal will have a boolean value, a source category, and a reason code. The combined classifications will be derived only after the individual signals have been audited.

## Planned classification bounds

E2 will report at least three classifications:

- Explicit lower bound: positive only when an auditable, high-specificity marker directly supports agent involvement for the relevant creation or maintenance event.
- Expanded marker estimate: includes validated lower-specificity markers that meet the hand-label precision threshold.
- Upper-bound classifier estimate: includes plausible agent-like cases captured by a validated classifier, while clearly identifying false-positive risk and the assumptions behind the upper bound.

Creation and maintenance will be reported independently. A skill may therefore be human-like at creation and agent-like in maintenance, agent-like at creation and human-like in maintenance, positive for both, or unknown.

## Execution plan

### Step 1 - inventory available history

Inspect the full raw schema and quantify availability of first/last commit timestamps, author identifiers, commit messages, commit counts, repository metadata, and history linkage. Reconcile those counts with E0 Gate A and document the history-available denominator before classification. Do not expose raw author names in logs, tables, or committed documentation.

### Step 2 - define marker dictionaries and normalization

Create versioned, double-anonymous marker specifications for identity, message, content, and repository/workflow evidence. Normalize case, Unicode, email-domain syntax, bot suffixes, trailers, and common generated-by phrasing. Keep high-specificity and broad candidate markers in separate columns. Every match must receive a non-identifying reason code.

### Step 3 - build event-level authorship evidence

For each representative with history, construct an event-level table containing only anonymized identifiers, event order, event date, event position (`first`, `middle`, `last`), signal columns, and reason codes. Derive first-event and last-event evidence without retaining author names in committed artifacts. Preserve an explicit no-history and insufficient-history status.

### Step 4 - define creation and maintenance outcomes

Create separate columns for:

- `creation_agent_explicit`;
- `maintenance_agent_explicit`;
- `maintenance_agent_any_event`;
- `creation_human_like` and `maintenance_human_like` only when supported by validated evidence;
- `authorship_class_lower`, `authorship_class_expanded`, and `authorship_class_upper`;
- `authorship_unknown` and `history_quality`.

Do not collapse human absence of an agent marker into proof of human authorship.

### Step 5 - hand-label validation

Draw a deterministic, anonymized sample stratified by creation/maintenance position, cohort, language from E1, location class, text length, copy/template/bulk flags, and marker status. Hand-label whether each available signal supports agent involvement, human involvement, both/ambiguous, or unknown. Estimate precision, recall, agreement, and Wilson confidence intervals for each marker family. Use the results to set the lower-bound and expanded-marker rules.

Execution note: the review used only anonymized reason codes and categories. The implemented sample was partitioned by event position, cohort, language, text-length bucket, and marker status; location class and catalog/template/bulk flags were retained as review metadata rather than used as additional partition dimensions. Explicit automated/high-specificity identity or explicit generated/authored-by agent evidence was labeled `agent_involvement`; co-authorship evidence without an identifiable agent claim was labeled `both_or_ambiguous`; generic automation and no decisive marker were labeled `unknown`. Ambiguous cases were excluded from strict binary precision/recall rather than treated as human authorship.

### Step 6 - estimate creation and maintenance shares

Report counts and shares for the distinct-content primary population, separated into creation and maintenance. Break results down by the ten E0 cohorts, E1 language class, location class, copy/template/bulk strata, and history-quality category. Report denominators, no-history rates, unknown rates, and confidence intervals for every estimate.

Execution note: estimates use the distinct representative population. Shares conditioned on usable history use the history-available denominator; all-population shares retain the 1,419,844 unknown-history representatives in the denominator. Wilson 95% intervals are reported for history-conditioned shares. The full language table, including low-frequency languages, is retained in the ignored Step 6 output.

### Step 7 - robustness and concentration checks

Repeat estimates with:

- explicit markers only versus validated expanded markers;
- first-event author versus last-event author versus any-event evidence;
- distinct-content versus copy-inclusive occurrences;
- repository contribution caps using E0 `repo_rank`;
- exclusions of catalog, template, and bulk-repository signals;
- dated/history-complete subsets versus all records with partial history.

Control for cohort and popularity when comparing classes. Treat the copy-inclusive and uncapped results as sensitivities, never as replacements for the distinct-content estimate.

Execution note: the repository concentration sensitivity uses the first 50 representative skills per repository by E0 `repo_rank`. Because Step 5 did not validate a lower-specificity marker family for expansion, the current expanded-marker sensitivity is explicitly recorded as identical to the explicit lower-bound definition rather than silently introducing a new rule.

### Step 8 - report the range and limitations

The final result must state a lower bound, expanded estimate, and upper-bound estimate separately for creation and maintenance. Explain how incomplete histories, human-committed agent work, marker false positives, repository concentration, and cohort date bias affect interpretation. Include a table of excluded/unknown cases and a marker audit appendix.

Execution note: Step 8 reports the validated explicit lower bound, an expanded estimate equal to the lower bound because no lower-specificity rule met the validation threshold, and a clearly marked theoretical ceiling because no empirical upper-bound classifier was validated. This is a documented limitation, not an imputed estimate.

## Acceptance criteria before E3

- History-availability denominators reconcile with E0.
- All marker families have versioned definitions and reason codes.
- No raw author names, emails, or repository-owner names appear in committed files.
- Hand-label validation is complete and reports per-marker performance.
- Creation and maintenance are not conflated.
- Lower, expanded, and upper estimates are all reported with unknown/history coverage.
- Distinct-content, copy-inclusive, and repository-capped sensitivity results are reproducible.

The empirical upper-bound-classifier criterion was not met: the final report therefore uses a theoretical ceiling and explicitly identifies this as an E2 limitation. Workflow/repository marker rules are versioned but were not included in the executed representative-level evidence because the available pipeline did not expose a validated per-skill workflow signal.

## Scope boundary

E2 estimates authorship and maintenance evidence only. Structural convergence belongs to E3, phrasing convergence to E4, topic coverage and semantic diversity to E5, and integration/moderation by authorship and language to E6.
