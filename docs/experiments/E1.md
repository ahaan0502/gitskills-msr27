# E1 - Natural-language distribution

Status: complete. See [E1 results](../results/E1-results.md) for all measurements and output summaries.

## Project-plan objective

Detect the language of each skill's description, body prose, and paragraphs. Report language shares overall and by monthly cohort, test whether descriptions and bodies use the same language, and examine sensitivity to copying and E0 repository/content flags.

## Research questions

- What languages appear in GitSkills, and what share of distinct skills does each represent?
- Do language shares change across the monthly cohorts defined in E0?
- Does a skill's description use the same language as its body prose?
- Are language distributions sensitive to copy frequency, catalog/template repositories, or bulk repositories?
- How reliable is the detector on a stratified reference-labeled sample?

## Unit of analysis

- Primary unit: one row per distinct `file_sha` in the E0 skills table.
- Primary population: all distinct contents for overall estimates; E0-valid dated representatives for cohort estimates.
- Copy-inclusive sensitivity: retain all artifact occurrences and report separately from distinct-content estimates.
- Cohorts: earliest valid in-window first-commit month. Undated and pre-window records remain excluded or explicitly categorized rather than silently assigned a month.

## Methodology

1. Use `fm_description` for descriptions and `body_prose` for body prose. Reconstruct paragraph blocks from representative raw content because the flattened body field does not preserve blank-line boundaries.
2. Normalize Unicode and trim detector inputs. Treat null/blank values as missing. Exclude empty, symlink-like, and parser-error records from primary field estimates. Retain short, mixed, and unknown outcomes with explicit status columns.
3. Pin `lingua-language-detector==2.2.0`, use its bundled models and all supported languages, and set minimum relative distance to 0.15. Retain relative confidence without interpreting it as a calibrated probability.
4. Detect descriptions, bodies, and paragraphs independently. Store predicted language, confidence, status, and paragraph-level results in an ignored derived table.
5. Aggregate complete overall shares with explicit denominators and unknown/undetermined categories.
6. Aggregate shares by the ten E0-valid monthly cohorts with counts and unknown rates.
7. Calculate description/body agreement, disagreement matrices, unknown/short-text rates, and paragraph-level mixed-language rates.
8. Repeat description/body summaries for copy-inclusive occurrences and true/false catalog, template, and bulk-repository flag strata. Keep these as sensitivity analyses.
9. Validate detector behavior using a deterministic, anonymized hand-label sample stratified by field, cohort, text length, and selected predicted labels. Retrieve selected text only after metadata sampling, and report agreement, per-label precision/recall where supported, and a plausible uncertainty range.
10. Consolidate commands, software versions, denominators, exclusions, tables, limitations, and output inventory in the committed results document.

## Text-quality decisions

- Description short-text threshold: fewer than 20 Unicode characters.
- Body short-text threshold: fewer than 50 Unicode characters.
- No description-to-body language imputation.
- Unknown, missing, excluded, short-text, and mixed-language statuses remain visible in denominators and audit outputs.
- Paragraphs exclude headings, fenced code, and empty blocks.

## Threats and controls

- Short descriptions may be impossible to classify; retain unknown rather than forcing a label.
- Skills may intentionally mix languages; preserve paragraph labels and report mixed-language content.
- Front matter can create artificial similarity; analyze description and body separately.
- Copy-heavy and bulk repositories can distort prevalence; use distinct contents as primary and label occurrence/flag views as sensitivities.
- Cohort history is biased toward canonical locations; report coverage limitations from E0.
